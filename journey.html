THE PORT JOURNEY

Every movement has a next step.

Follow the complete port-call story, from the first arrival message to the onward cargo handover. These nine stages explain the key interfaces in a vessel call.

01

Vessel arrival & pilotage

The call begins before the vessel reaches the port. Arrival information creates the first shared picture of the ship, its purpose and its expected timing.

↗02

Mooring & safety coordination

Alongside is the beginning of a new working environment, where vessel, terminal and service-provider activities meet.

↗03

Unloading & cargo discharge

Discharge connects the voyage to the shore-side cargo chain. Its progress influences storage, collection and the vessel’s remaining programme.

↗04

Yard operations & storage

After discharge, cargo may spend time within a terminal or storage arrangement before its next movement. That interval needs a plan of its own.

↗05

Customs & inspections

Documentation and inspection interfaces can influence when cargo or the vessel can move to its next stage. They deserve early attention within the plan.

↗06

Inland transport & gate operations

The onward movement turns a port arrival into delivery to a business, warehouse or project site.

↗07

Loading operations for departure

Export loading brings cargo, vessel planning and terminal activity together before the next voyage.

↗08

Final checks & departure

A good departure handover gathers the call into a clear account of completed work and remaining matters.

↗09

Connecting the wider trade journey

The ninth idea in the reference describes the impact of the whole process: ports connect businesses and markets through a chain of coordinated movements.

↗

A journey shaped around the actual vessel.

Container, bulk, tanker and commercial calls have different requirements. The sequence is illustrative; stages may overlap or change with the assignment.
