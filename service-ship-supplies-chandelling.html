Ship supplies & chandelling

Shipboard life depends on a steady supply of ordinary essentials. Food, consumables, cleaning materials and working stores may not dominate a voyage plan, but their absence becomes immediately visible. A well-prepared supply order connects the crew’s needs, the vessel schedule and delivery conditions. Good chandelling begins with an accurate list and ends with a useful receipt and a clear record of any substitutions.

01

What a ship supply requirement can contain

A supply brief can cover provisions, cabin stores, deck stores and other consumables. Fresh and frozen products, dry goods, cleaning supplies, personal-use items and working materials each raise different questions about specification, packaging, storage and delivery. Technical components should be identified separately from general stores to avoid ambiguity.

Requests for safety, medical or regulated items need particular care. The enquiry should state the exact requirement and the responsible onboard or technical contact. Availability should never be treated as proof of suitability. Where a product requires certification, documentation or professional approval, those expectations belong in the brief.

02

How restocking is planned

The vessel team provides quantities, specifications and the intended delivery window. The scope is checked for unclear units, missing product descriptions and permitted substitutions. The delivery route is then considered against port access, vessel position and the practical means of getting the goods aboard.

The useful milestone is not simply “vehicle dispatched.” It is delivery to the agreed receiving point, followed by a check against the order. Exceptions should be recorded: missing items, changed quantities, damaged packaging or agreed alternatives. A clear acceptance record helps the crew and purchasing team reconcile the supply.

03

A provision list should reflect the people aboard

Crew size, voyage duration, available storage and dietary needs all influence a sensible order. A shopping list copied from a previous voyage may overlook a different crew complement or a longer time between calls. Fresh products need a delivery plan that respects their handling and storage requirements.

The aim is a practical balance: enough information to source suitable goods without making unsupported assumptions about onboard preferences. If a substitution is acceptable, state the boundaries. If it is not acceptable, make that equally clear before the delivery is arranged.

04

What to provide before we arrange supply

Send the vessel particulars, port or terminal, estimated arrival, delivery deadline and a readable order list. Use consistent units and identify items that are critical to departure or continuing operations. Include the receiving officer and purchasing approval contact.

Where the vessel is at anchorage or a location with additional access requirements, flag that at the beginning. The route to the ship is part of the supply assignment, not an afterthought. A realistic delivery plan also needs room for the vessel’s working schedule and any required permissions.

05

Why choose coordinated provisioning

Good provisioning respects both the budget and the onboard reality. A low headline price can be a poor outcome if the quantity is wrong, a substitution is unsuitable or a delivery misses the receiving window. The value of coordination is the link between specification, supply and acceptance.

Your initial enquiry checklist

  • Provision/store list with units and quantities
  • Crew complement and relevant dietary requirements
  • Product specifications and substitution permissions
  • Delivery location and vessel receiving contact
  • Budget approval and deadline

Questions, answered.

Can you restock a vessel during a short port call?

That depends on the order, sourcing lead time, vessel location and access arrangements. Share the list and schedule early for confirmation.

Will substitutes be supplied automatically?

Substitution permissions should be agreed with the purchasing or onboard team. Critical specifications should be stated clearly.
